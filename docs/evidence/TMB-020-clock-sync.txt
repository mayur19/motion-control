Live protocol/pipeline integration
----------------------------------
Command:
  godot --headless --path apps/host-godot \
    --script res://tests/clock_service_integration_test.gd
Result:
  TMB-020_CLOCK_INTEGRATION_OK offset_ms=50.470 rtt_ms=0.060

This runs HELLO/WELCOME, host PING, controller PONG and MOTION over real local
UDP sockets. It proves immediate post-pair synchronization, correct four-time
handling, controller-ID association, conversion into host monotonic time, and
adjusted sample-age calculation in the ingest pipeline.

Controller implementations
--------------------------
iOS keeps its paired Network.framework UDP control path open and answers PING
with DispatchTime monotonic timestamps. Android retains its paired DatagramSocket
on a dedicated control thread and answers with System.nanoTime timestamps. Both
validate session/controller identity before replying. iOS has 12 passing tests;
Android unit tests and debug assembly pass.

Physical iPhone evidence
------------------------
A 639-second physical iPhone 17e stream produced 128 clock-sync observations
over 10.5 minutes. The resulting plot is:

  docs/evidence/TMB-020-iphone-clock-stability.svg

The plot shows a 3.093 ms controller-offset range and 6.314 ms median clock
exchange RTT. Eight early observations retain the previously discovered
-198,804 ppm estimator artifact; they are visibly clipped and annotated rather
than omitted. That artifact led to the one-second forward-time drift-baseline
guard and regression test described above. The physical run log SHA-256 is:

  f2d657cfa1ca3c48f657759759c51deba25e888db4afe7253d5d61da246dbc91

The plot is reproducible with:

    docs/evidence/TMB-020-iphone-clock-stability.svg

An Android physical stability plot remains pending until the Galaxy reference

Android time-base defect (physical Galaxy M14 5G, 2026-11-06)
-------------------------------------------------------------
The Android controller mixed time bases: SensorEvent.timestamp
(elapsedRealtimeNanos) against PONG times from System.nanoTime(). On a phone that
had slept about 18.4 h since boot, every sample converted to about 66,355 s in
the future. Fixed by protocol/ProtocolClock.kt (elapsedRealtimeNanos for every
wire timestamp); physical post-fix median sample age is 8.3 ms. See
TMB-016-017-android-motion.txt. The spec's "drop if more than 20 ms in the
future" rule would otherwise have discarded every Android sample.
